Wallet Intelligence Dashboard

Correlating crypto exchange reports across accounts. Takes one master exchange report and any number of sub-reports, finds the counterparties they share, and reports the value that actually moved — not the value counted twice.

The problem

Comparing exchange reports account by account produces two kinds of wrong answer. A withdrawal in one report and the matching deposit in another are one movement of money recorded twice — sum both and every total inflates. And a plain overlap check tells you two accounts touched the same counterparty, not which direction the value went.

What I built

The dashboard resolves every column by alias, so a renamed header does not break a run, and matches counterparties per analysis mode: withdrawal, deposit, Binance Pay, attempted withdrawal, P2P, a combined deposits-and-withdrawals mode, and a custom mode where you pick the sheet and column. Transfers seen from both sides are matched on transaction ID and counted once — but the duplicate row is kept, because that pairing is the strongest evidence the two accounts are linked. Failed, cancelled and rejected rows are excluded rather than summed as settled.

Inside it

01

De-duplication of two-sided transfers

A withdrawal and its matching deposit are one movement; matching transaction IDs are counted once and the pairing is surfaced as evidence.

02

Direction, not just volume

Every counterparty gets gross in, gross out and net, so a wallet that took 50k in and sent 50k out does not read as nothing.

03

Cluster graph

A bipartite account-to-counterparty graph finds sub-to-sub links the master account never touched.

04

Pattern detection

Repeated identical amounts, amounts sitting just under a round number, and two accounts hitting one counterparty within a short window.

05

Readable identities

Each report’s UID and email are read from its customer information sheet, so tables name people instead of filenames.

06

Data quality tab

What loaded, what was skipped and what each filter removed — visible rather than assumed.

Outcome

  • Totals that reflect the value that actually moved between accounts.
  • Links between sub-accounts that a per-file review would never surface.
